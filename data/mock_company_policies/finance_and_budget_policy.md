Currency in this document is the fictional currency unit (FCU). Amounts are illustrative.

## 1. Purpose

This document caps retention discounts. It does not say that a discount improves retention. No experiment in this project measured that.

## 2. Budget

- The fictional monthly retention budget is 25,000 FCU.
- ChurnShield does not keep a live ledger of offers already promised.
- Every recommendation must say that budget headroom is unverified.
- A recommendation inside an employee’s discount limit is not a payment approval.

## 3. Authority and caps

- Per-customer cap: 25 FCU off the bill in any one month. If a percent would exceed 25 FCU, reduce the monthly amount to 25 FCU and require a manager.
- Employee monthly discount authority: 8 FCU. A monthly discount above 8 FCU needs a manager.
- A manager cannot exceed 15% or 3 months. Those limits are in the retention offer policy.
- One-year exceptions and Tier C always need a manager, even when the monthly amount is 8 FCU or less.
- Employees cannot approve a discount on a one-year or two-year contract.

## 4. Examples

Employee authority. A 5% discount on MonthlyCharges of 60 is 3.00 FCU. That is at or under 8 FCU.

Manager authority. A 10% discount on MonthlyCharges of 90 is 9.00 FCU. That is above 8 FCU.

Cap. A 15% discount on MonthlyCharges of 200 would be 30 FCU. The offered monthly amount is 25 FCU, and a manager must approve it.

## 5. What this policy does not allow

Do not create a new percent, extend the duration, or waive the cap because the churn probability is high. Do not describe the customer as high-value in order to justify a larger discount.
